01 · What was asked, what was built

Does the 4680 format change how runaway spreads, and which resistance controls it?

The tabless 4680 is usually discussed electrically. The question here was thermal: in a pack of large cells, what decides whether one cell's failure propagates? The base model is COMSOL's Thermal Runaway Propagation in a Battery Pack (Battery Design Module), built on the simplified model of Coman, Darcy and White (J. Electrochem. Soc. 169, 040516, 2022). It was adapted to 4680 geometry and energy, not rebuilt.

ItemValue
The swept parameter, stated preciselyIn COMSOL's spirally wound Battery Layers node, kT_batt_tl is the through-layer conductivity: across the windings, the radial direction of a cylindrical cell, stock 1 W/m·K. kT_batt_il, along the foils (azimuthal and axial, stock 30 W/m·K), was never changed. The sweep varied the through-layer value. It is not a direct tabless knob: tabless changes how the foil edges connect to the cap, not the bulk conductivity across the layers.
What is and is not in the repositoryIn: the written record, the run matrix as data, a Python package that reproduces the scaling arguments and generates every figure, a reduced-order surrogate built here, and a claim-by-claim audit. Not in: the COMSOL .mph file, mesh, logs or field data. The work was done on a university machine and only the written record survives.
02 · The run matrix COMSOL, transcribed

Twelve one-hour studies. One sharp line between cascade and arrest.

Rows are base cooling hplate, columns are through-layer conductivity ktl. Scroll through the four phases; the phase map follows.

Phase 1 · Cap contact resistance

Tabbed Rcap = 10⁻³ against tabless 10⁻⁵ m²·K/W on the pack floor with hplate = 2,000. Second ignition 22.6 against 22.9 min: a 100× change moved onset by about 20 s out of 23 min. The acrylic floor's own resistance swamps the contact. Null result.

Phase 2 · Through-layer sweep

At hplate = 2,000: ktl = 1.0 cascaded (second ignition 22.9 min, about 13 further cells by 52 min). 1.25, 1.5 and 2.0 stopped at cell 1. A 25 % change in internal conductivity flipped the outcome.

Phase 3 · Is it a cooling artefact?

At hplate = 500 the threshold did not move: 1.0 cascaded at 22.4 min, 1.25 and 1.5 stopped. With Bi ≈ 11 to 46 the inside of the cell, not the plate, is the bottleneck.

Phase 4 · Adiabatic stress test

At the threshold value 1.25: no cooling at all cascaded (second ignition about 26.9 min, pack average about 260 °C at the end); h = 100 arrested. Near the threshold a cell needs both an internal conduction path and a heat sink. The h = 0, k = 1.0 cell was inferred, never run.

03 · Why the threshold exists, and the numbers that check it

A neighbour triggers on its hottest point, and that point is the skin facing the burning cell.

With low through-layer conductivity, arriving heat piles up at that skin and the local maximum crosses 80 °C. With higher conductivity it spreads inward and down to the cooled base, and the maximum stays below 80 °C. The transition is sharp because the trigger is a step. It is analogous to a thermal-explosion tipping point, but the model has no Arrhenius kinetics, so real self-heating would make it softer and probably shift it.

Biot numbers of 2.3, 11.5 and 46 for h = 100, 500 and 2,000 (L = 23 mm, k = 1) say why: once Bi is well above 1, internal conduction limits heat removal and a stronger plate cannot pull heat out faster than the cell conducts it.
Null result on contact resistanceA 100× change in Rcap at the pack floor moved propagation onset by about 20 s in 23 min. The cooled boundary sits behind a few millimetres of acrylic (k ≈ 0.19 W/m·K, about 5 × 10⁻³ to 10⁻² m²·K/W), larger than the tabbed contact resistance. The correct tabless test, contact resistance on each cell's own base face, was not run. This study did not show that tabless stops propagation.
04 · Reduced-order surrogate built here, not COMSOL

A thermal network simple enough to read in one sitting, asked the same question.

Each cell is a core plus five radial shells split into 16 sectors; radial links use ktl, azimuthal links use kil. Neighbours couple through still air, acrylic holder bridges and steel strips; the cells sit in a wrapped air compartment; base cooling acts on each footprint through the axial path, a 3 mm acrylic floor and 1/heff. Same 110 kJ pulse, same 80 °C maximum-temperature trigger, 0.1 s explicit steps for 60 min. One parameter, the inter-cell coupling (×3.0), was calibrated so the surrogate matches the COMSOL threshold at h = 2,000. The rest is prediction, and the hero animation above plays these traces.

Supports: a maximum-temperature trigger on a neighbour's skin gives a sharp threshold in ktl; the threshold is insensitive to base cooling between 500 and 2,000; removing the heat sink flips the threshold case back to cascade; base contact resistance is second-order behind the acrylic floor. Does not support: the absolute threshold position, the cascade timing or the cell count. A reading aid for the COMSOL result, not evidence of it.

05 · Limitations, stated plainly

Believe the trends and the mechanism. Calibrate before believing the absolute numbers.

  • Homogenised cells. Uniform anisotropic solids; no windings, hot spots or internal shorts. A resolved 4680 jelly roll was attempted: the swept mesh inverted and a free mesh passed 25 GB.
  • Fixed trigger, no chemistry. A prescribed 110 kJ pulse at 80 °C; no SEI, anode or cathode kinetics. The sharpness partly reflects this choice.
  • No venting, no radiation, no convection in the air gap. Heat crosses by conduction through air, holders and connectors.
  • Bottom cooling only, on the pack floor, as a simplified case. Not a replica of any production pack.
  • No experimental validation of the 4680 parameters, no mesh or time-step convergence study, no per-cell probes, one inferred matrix cell, untested sensitivities to trigger temperature, energy, pulse duration, gap and holder material.
